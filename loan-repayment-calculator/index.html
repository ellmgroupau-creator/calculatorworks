How the monthly repayment is calculated

This model assumes a fully amortising loan, a fixed annual rate and payments at the end of each month. Each payment covers the month’s interest and reduces the remaining principal. Interest takes a larger share of early payments.

Repayment formula

M = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

P is the amount borrowed; r is the annual percentage rate ÷ 100 ÷ 12; n is the term in years × 12. At 0% interest, M = P ÷ n. Total interest = M × n − P. The model treats the entered rate as a nominal annual interest rate, excluding fees; a fee-inclusive APR may not reproduce a lender’s payment.

Worked repayment example

Borrowing $500,000 at 6% for 30 years gives 360 payments of approximately $2,997.75, total payments of $1,079,190.95 and interest of $579,190.95 before fees. Calculations use the unrounded payment; a lender may round each instalment.

What the estimate leaves out

The payment covers principal and interest only. Add property taxes, insurance, mortgage insurance, fees and other ownership expenses separately. Daily interest, local compounding conventions, changing rates and payment dates can change a lender’s figures. Extra repayments require a separate payoff calculation.

Why can my lender show a different amount?

A lender may use daily accrual, a different compounding convention, fees or a different payment date. Check the contract rate and repayment schedule rather than treating this estimate as a quote.
