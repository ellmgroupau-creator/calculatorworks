The equation behind the resting-energy estimate

The resting-energy estimate uses the Mifflin–St Jeor equation: 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age (years) + s, where s is +5 for the male equation or −161 for the female equation. The result is kilocalories per day. This predicts resting energy expenditure; it does not measure your metabolism.

Worked example

For an 80 kg, 180 cm, 35-year-old adult using the male equation: 800 + 1,125 − 175 + 5 = 1,755 kcal/day. Using the female equation gives 1,589 kcal/day. If an activity factor of 1.55 is applied, the male estimate becomes 2,720.25 kcal/day.

BMR, resting energy and daily energy are different

A resting estimate excludes most movement and exercise. The TDEE and calorie tools multiply the resting estimate by an activity factor. The available factors are 1.2 (sedentary), 1.375 (light), 1.55 (moderate), 1.725 (very active) and 1.9 (exceptionally active). These are broad planning conventions, not individual measurements or part of the original resting-energy equation.

Who should interpret this cautiously?

These adult estimates are not a calorie prescription and are not intended for children, pregnancy, breastfeeding or clinical nutrition. Body composition, illness and training can affect accuracy. Use an appropriately qualified clinician or dietitian for individual dietary decisions rather than treating the resting number as a target intake.

Method source

Mifflin et al. (1990): A new predictive equation for resting energy expenditure in healthy individuals. The cited study supports the resting equation, not the activity multipliers.
