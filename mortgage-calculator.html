How the monthly repayment is calculated

This model assumes a fully amortising loan, a fixed annual rate and payments at the end of each month. Each payment covers the month’s interest and reduces the remaining principal. Interest takes a larger share of early payments.

Repayment formula

M = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

P is the amount borrowed; r is the annual percentage rate ÷ 100 ÷ 12; n is the term in years × 12. At 0% interest, M = P ÷ n. Total interest = M × n − P. The model treats the entered rate as a nominal annual interest rate, excluding fees; a fee-inclusive APR may not reproduce a lender’s payment.

Worked repayment example

Borrowing $500,000 at 6% for 30 years gives 360 payments of approximately $2,997.75, total payments of $1,079,190.95 and interest of $579,190.95 before fees. Calculations use the unrounded payment; a lender may round each instalment.

What the estimate leaves out

The payment covers principal and interest only. Add property taxes, insurance, mortgage insurance, fees and other ownership expenses separately. Daily interest, local compounding conventions, changing rates and payment dates can change a lender’s figures. Extra repayments require a separate payoff calculation.

Why can my lender show a different amount?

A lender may use daily accrual, a different compounding convention, fees or a different payment date. Check the contract rate and repayment schedule rather than treating this estimate as a quote.

How Mortgage Repayments Work

Your mortgage repayment is typically made up of principal and interest. The principal is the amount borrowed, while the interest is the cost charged by the lender. Mortgage repayments are influenced by your loan amount, loan term, interest rate, repayment frequency, and whether the loan is fixed or variable.

Factors That Affect Your Mortgage Payment

  • Loan amount and deposit size
  • Interest rate changes
  • Loan term length
  • Property taxes and insurance
  • Extra repayments and offset accounts

Example Mortgage Scenario

If you borrow $500,000 over 30 years at 6% interest, your estimated monthly repayment would be approximately $2,998 excluding taxes and fees. Paying extra repayments can significantly reduce the total interest paid over the life of the loan.

Common Mortgage Mistakes

  • Borrowing beyond your comfortable budget
  • Ignoring rate rise scenarios
  • Not comparing lenders
  • Forgetting additional property ownership costs

How Mortgage Costs Change Over Time

Mortgage repayments are heavily influenced by interest rates, loan length, property taxes, insurance, and repayment frequency. Even small changes in rates can significantly affect long-term borrowing costs.

Fixed vs Variable Rate Considerations

Fixed-rate loans provide repayment stability, while variable-rate loans may fluctuate depending on market conditions and lender adjustments.

Why Extra Repayments Matter

Additional repayments can reduce total interest paid and shorten loan duration, particularly during the early years of a mortgage.
