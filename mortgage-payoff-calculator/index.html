How extra repayments reduce interest

Interest is calculated on the remaining balance each month. Paying more principal now reduces the balance on which later interest is charged. The model keeps the rate and payment constant, charges monthly interest, and caps the final payment at the amount owed.

Calculation method

Monthly interest = opening balance × annual rate ÷ 100 ÷ 12. Principal repaid = payment − monthly interest. Closing balance = opening balance − principal repaid. The model repeats until the loan is repaid, up to 1,200 months. Where a term and extra-payment field are shown, the normal amortising payment is calculated first and the extra amount is added.

Zero-interest example

A $120,000 balance with 10 years remaining at 0% has a normal monthly payment of $1,000. Adding $250 per month repays the balance in 96 months, saving 24 months. There is no interest to save in this example.

Before making extra payments

Check prepayment limits and fees, and confirm the lender applies extra money to principal. Rate changes, offset accounts, daily accrual and payment holidays are excluded. A payment that does not cover monthly interest cannot repay the loan under this model.
