Interest rates: compare like with like

Start with the rate convention

A percentage alone does not describe the cost or growth of money. Record the period, whether interest compounds, and which fees are included before comparing two offers. A monthly rate is not an annual rate. An advertised effective annual rate is also different from a nominal annual rate divided into monthly periods.

Worked example: the same 6% label

For 10,000 held for one year, simple interest at 6% adds 600. Monthly compounding at a nominal 6% uses 0.5% each month: 10,000 × (1.005)^12 = 10,616.78. The effective annual increase is 6.1678%, not exactly 6%. These examples exclude deposits, withdrawals, fees and taxes.

How to enter a rate

Our compound-interest tool expects a nominal annual percentage and compounds monthly. Enter 6 for 6%, not 0.06. To model a 6% effective annual yield with monthly compounding, the equivalent nominal rate is 12 × ((1.06)^(1/12) − 1) × 100, approximately 5.8411%. Do not enter a fee-inclusive borrowing APR as a loan interest rate unless the provider confirms that convention matches the model.

Check the quotation

For a real offer, compare the same amount and term, the total payable, fixed or variable terms, fees, and repayment timing. Rate labels and required disclosures vary by country. A calculator illustrates a stated assumption; it does not establish the contractual cost.
