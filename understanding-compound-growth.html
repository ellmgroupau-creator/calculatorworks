Compound growth: separate deposits from earnings

What the model measures

Compound growth adds earnings to a balance so that later earnings are calculated on a different starting amount. The final balance contains both money you supplied and modelled growth. Comparing final balances without separating those parts can make an increase in contributions look like a better investment return.

Worked example: 10 years

Start with 10,000 and assume a constant nominal annual rate of 6%. With annual compounding and no deposits, 10,000 × 1.06^10 = 17,908.48. With monthly compounding, 10,000 × 1.005^120 = 18,193.97. The difference comes from the compounding convention, not a different starting balance. Our calculator uses the monthly version.

Add regular deposits

Adding 100 at the end of every month gives 34,581.90 after the same 120 months. You supplied 22,000: the initial 10,000 plus 12,000 in deposits. Modelled growth is 12,581.90. End-of-month deposits begin earning in the following month. Beginning-of-month deposits would produce a different answer; do not compare them as identical scenarios.

Use scenarios, not a forecast

Enter the initial balance, nominal annual rate, years and optional monthly deposit. Keep the currency consistent. Change one input at a time to understand its effect. The model rounds time to whole months and assumes a constant rate; markets do not follow a smooth path. Fees, tax, inflation and missed deposits are excluded. A higher assumed rate is not evidence that such a return is achievable.
