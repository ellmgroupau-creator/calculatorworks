Compare the order of debt repayment

Snowball directs spare money to the smallest remaining balance. Avalanche directs it to the highest annual rate. Both pay the entered minimums on other accounts and keep the same total monthly budget as debts disappear. With only one debt, both strategies are identical.

How to enter several debts

Use the optional debt list with one account per line: name, balance, annual rate %, minimum monthly payment. Enter plain numbers without currency symbols or thousands separators. When a list is supplied, the single-balance and single-rate fields are ignored; the payment field becomes your total monthly budget. Leave the list blank to calculate one debt.

Calculation assumptions

Each month the model adds balance × annual rate ÷ 100 ÷ 12 in interest, pays the entered minimum amounts, then allocates the rest of the budget in strategy order. Freed payments roll into the same budget. Minimums stay fixed until the final payment. No new spending, fees, rate changes or promotional periods are included. The simulation stops at 1,200 months.

Which strategy costs less?

For example, a $1,000 balance at 5% and a $5,000 balance at 20% point to different first targets: snowball starts with $1,000; avalanche starts with the 20% account. Under stable rates and the same budget, prioritising high-rate balances generally reduces interest. Compare both results rather than assuming the payoff order is the same.
