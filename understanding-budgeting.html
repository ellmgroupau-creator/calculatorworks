Build a monthly budget you can check

Put every amount on the same basis

A usable budget compares take-home income with spending, debt payments and planned savings for the same period. Start with actual statements rather than guesses. Convert weekly amounts to a monthly average using × 52 / 12; multiplying by four misses four weeks over a year. A monthly average helps planning, but bill due dates still matter for cash flow.

Worked example: find the unallocated balance

Suppose monthly take-home income is 4,500. Essential spending is 2,500, flexible spending 700, debt payments 400 and planned savings 500. The remaining amount is 4,500 − 2,500 − 700 − 400 − 500 = 400. This is an illustration, not a recommended allocation. If annual bills total another 1,200, reserve 100 per month for them and the remaining amount becomes 300.

Avoid double counting

A transfer between your own accounts is not new income. If you record purchases on a credit card as spending, do not count repayment of those same purchases as another expense. Separate repayment of older debt and interest from current purchases. Keep a consistent method so the budget reconciles with your account balances.

Review the gap each month

Compare planned amounts with actual spending, then identify the categories responsible for any gap. If income varies, test a lower-income month as well as the average. A positive budget balance is not automatically spare cash if an unpaid bill is due tomorrow. Use the savings-goal tool only with an amount the budget can realistically support.
