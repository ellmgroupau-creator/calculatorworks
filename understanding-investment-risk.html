Investment risk: what a growth estimate cannot show

A constant return is an assumption

A growth calculator applies the rate you enter; it does not assess whether an investment can earn that rate. Real returns can vary, and capital can be lost. Price volatility is only one risk. Also consider whether money can be accessed when needed, whether an issuer can pay, and whether rising prices reduce purchasing power.

Worked example: losses and recovery

A 20% fall takes 10,000 to 8,000. Recovering the lost 2,000 requires a 25% gain on 8,000, not a 20% gain. Even a +20% year followed by a −20% year leaves 9,600 from an initial 10,000. The arithmetic average return is zero, but the balance is down 4%. A smooth annual assumption hides this effect.

Check purchasing power and concentration

With a 5% nominal return and 3% inflation, the real change is (1.05 / 1.03) − 1 = approximately 1.94%, before fees and taxes. Separately, holding many assets that depend on the same business or market can leave substantial concentration risk. Diversification can reduce some risks but cannot guarantee a profit or prevent market losses.

Use the result as a question

Compare several rate assumptions, then ask what happens if the target date arrives during a loss. Money required on a fixed date has different constraints from money with a flexible horizon. Withdrawals also change the effect of the return sequence. This page explains arithmetic and risk concepts; it does not recommend an asset or a suitable personal allocation.
