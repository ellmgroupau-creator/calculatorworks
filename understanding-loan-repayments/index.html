Loan repayments: payment, interest and term

What a repayment includes

In a standard fully amortizing loan, each payment covers interest on the remaining balance and repays some principal. A fixed monthly payment does not mean the interest portion stays fixed. As the balance falls, less interest is due and more of the same payment reduces the debt.

Worked example: 25,000 over five years

At a nominal annual rate of 8%, paid monthly for 60 months, the modelled payment is 506.91. The first month’s interest is 25,000 × 0.08 / 12 = 166.67. About 340.24 of the payment reduces principal. Using the unrounded payment, total interest is approximately 5,414.59. Actual schedules can differ by cents because lenders round each payment and interest charge.

Compare terms with the same loan amount

Keep principal and rate unchanged, then change only the term. A longer term usually lowers the monthly payment but increases total interest in this fixed-rate model. Write down both figures. For extra payments, check whether the lender applies them to principal and whether early-repayment charges apply. An extra-payment calculation is not a replacement for those contract terms.

Inputs and limits

Use the amount actually financed, the nominal interest rate and the repayment term. Compare the model with a lender schedule before relying on it. Fees, insurance, taxes, daily interest, variable rates, balloon payments and payment holidays need separate treatment. At 0% interest the monthly payment is simply principal divided by the number of months.
