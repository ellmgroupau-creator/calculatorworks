What this withholding estimate means

This tool applies a percentage that you supply. It does not select a statutory tax rate, calculate progressive tax brackets or determine your final tax liability. Use a rate from payroll or the relevant tax authority. The $ symbol is a display convention; keep all money inputs in the same currency.

Withholding and take-home amount

Estimated withholding = taxable amount × rate ÷ 100. Estimated net amount = taxable amount − withholding, less any separately entered deductions. For the self-employed tool, taxable profit is income minus entered expenses; deductibility is not verified.

Worked withholding example

A $5,000 bonus at a supplied withholding rate of 22% has estimated withholding of $1,100 and an estimated net amount of $3,900 before other deductions. The 22% is an example input, not a recommendation for your country or circumstances.

Why withholding differs from final tax

Withholding is money set aside or collected in advance. Your final liability may depend on annual income, residency, tax credits, social contributions, student loans and other income. Do not use this flat-percentage estimate to file a return or assume it reproduces a payslip.
