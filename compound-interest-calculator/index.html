What is compounded in this estimate?

The calculator treats the annual rate as nominal and compounds monthly. Interest is added to the balance each month, so later interest is earned on earlier interest. If a monthly contribution field is present, contributions are added at the end of each month.

Formula and contribution timing

FV = P(1 + r)n + C((1 + r)n − 1) ÷ r

P is the starting balance, r = annual rate ÷ 100 ÷ 12, n = years × 12 and C is the monthly contribution (zero when no contribution field is shown). At 0%, FV = P + C × n. Terms are modelled in whole months.

Worked example

$10,000 at a nominal annual rate of 6%, compounded monthly for 10 years without further deposits, grows to approximately $18,193.97. The estimated growth is $8,193.97. With $100 deposited at each month-end, the balance is about $34,581.90.

Reading the result

Subtract your starting money and deposits from the final balance to find the growth. Taxes, investment fees, inflation and market fluctuations are excluded. A constant positive return is an illustration, not a forecast or guarantee. An effective annual yield is different from the nominal rate used here.

Compound interest planning notes

Compound interest allows your money to grow faster because interest is earned on both the original balance and previously earned interest. Over long periods, compound growth can dramatically increase savings and investment balances.

How Investors Use Compound Interest

  • Retirement planning
  • Investment growth forecasting
  • Savings goal tracking
  • Long-term wealth building

Example

If you invest $10,000 at 7% annual growth and continue reinvesting returns, the balance can more than double over time due to compounding.
